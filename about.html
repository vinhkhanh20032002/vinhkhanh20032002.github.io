Tell me about yourself

I'm Khanh Vo, a final-year Information Security student at the University of South Florida, graduating in December 2026. I hold CompTIA A+ and Network+ certifications and am currently working toward Security+. Alongside coursework, I built and documented a multi-phase home SOC lab that includes an OPNsense firewall, a Wazuh SIEM, Suricata IDS, Pi-hole DNS filtering, and a segmented VLAN network for Trusted, Lab, Infrastructure, and IoT traffic. Before focusing fully on cybersecurity, I worked as a cable assembler at Lockheed Martin and provided part-time IT support at a dental office, which sharpened my attention to detail and my comfort troubleshooting under pressure. I'm bilingual in Vietnamese and English, and I learn best through hands-on labs and simulations rather than passive study — which is why I keep rebuilding and breaking my own lab environment to understand it from the inside out.

What are you working toward after graduation?

After graduation, I'm working toward an entry-level SOC Analyst role, with longer-term interest in cloud security and application security. I'm following a structured certification roadmap — Security+, Network+, CySA+, Microsoft SC-200, and BTL1 — while using my home SOC lab as hands-on proof of the skills those certifications describe: monitoring logs in Wazuh, tuning detections in Suricata, and defending a segmented network the way I would in a real environment. I'm applying this outlook to my job search, sending tailored resumes for entry-level SOC and analyst roles and building a portfolio site to walk employers through my lab work. This path also reflects where the field is heading: the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (2025) projects 29% growth in information security analyst employment from 2024 to 2034, with roughly 16,000 openings a year — a strong signal that the entry-level effort I'm putting in now will pay off.
